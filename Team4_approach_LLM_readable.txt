1 ResearchDesign

1.1ResearchQuestion
Doesthetypeofpersuasiveappealahumanuses—   empathy-basedoreconomic/instrumental
— change how an LLM negotiates and prices a deal?

1.2DesignType
This is a between-subjects experiment:  each participant is randomly placed into only one
condition,sowecomparedifferentgroupsofpeopleratherthanaskingthesamepersontotry
both styles.

1.3Conditions

 •   Condition A — Empathy Appeal:    the participant is told to negotiate using personal cir-
   cumstances, goodwill, and relationship-building language (e.g., explaining why the dis-
   count matters to them).
 •   Condition B — Economic/Instrumental Appeal:     the participant is told to negotiate us-
   ing rational, self-interest-based arguments (e.g., market price comparisons, bulk/loyalty
   discounts, or the option to buy elsewhere).
 •   Condition C — Neutral/Control (optional):     the participant negotiates with no specific
   style instruction, giving a baseline to compare both styles against.

1.4WhatWeMeasure(DependentVariables)
 •   Finalnegotiatedprice— the main outcome of interest.
 •   Concessionrate— how much the LLM reduced its price relative to where it started.
 •   Numberandsizeofconcessions, andhowmanyturns it took to reach a deal.
 •   Whetheradealwasreachedatall.
 • The LLM’s   toneandbehavioralpattern(e.g., firm, accommodating, reciprocating).

1.5Participant-LevelCovariates

Since people differ naturally, we also record a few background traits to make sure any differ-
ences we see are really caused by the appeal type, and not just by who happened to land in
which group: trait empathy, trust in AI, prior negotiation experience, and prior familiarity
withLLMs.

1.6KeepingtheLLMConstant
For the comparison to be fair, everything about the LLM must stay exactly the same across
everysingleparticipantandcondition—thesamemodel,thesamesellerpersonaandsystem
prompt, the same starting price, the same generation settings, and the same turn limit.  The
only thing allowed to differ between participants is the assigned appeal style.  We also give
the LLM a hidden reservation price — the lowest price it is allowed to accept, invisible to
theparticipant—sothenegotiationhasrealstakesinsteadoftheLLMsimplygivingtheitem
away.

1.7Hypotheses

 •   H1: Empathy appeals will lead to a lower final price and larger concessions than economic
    appeals.
 •   H2: Thetwoappealstyleswilldiffernotjustinthefinalprice,butinwhenconcessionshap-
    pen during the conversation (e.g., empathy appeals producing earlier, gradual softening).
 •   H3: The effect of appeal type on final price will still hold even after accounting for each
    participant’s empathy, trust, and experience levels.

2 MaterialsandParticipantFlow

2.1WhatNeedstoBeBuiltFirst

A single fixed negotiation scenario (e.g., “negotiate the price of a used laptop listed at $500”);
a fixed LLM system prompt containing the seller persona and hidden reservation price; two
(or three) short instruction scripts, one per condition; a chat interface that automatically saves
every message as it happens; a short pre-survey and post-survey; and a brief plain-language
note telling participants what the study involves.

2.2WhatEachParticipantActuallyDoes

 1.Reads a short note about the study and agrees to take part.
 2.Fills a quick pre-survey: basic demographics, a short empathy scale, and a short AI-trust
    scale (∼ 2 minutes).
 3.Is randomly and invisibly placed into a condition by the system.
 4.Reads their condition’s instructions on how to frame their negotiation.
 5.Chats live with the LLM to try to get the best price, up to about 10 back-and-forth turns
    (∼ 5–10 minutes).
 6.Fills a short post-survey about fairness, satisfaction, and what kind of argument they felt
    they used (∼ 3 minutes).

Total time per participant: roughly 10–15 minutes.

4 SampleSize

The target is 100–200 participants  in total.  With two conditions, that works out to roughly
50–100 people per group; with three conditions, roughly 35–70 per group. As a rule of thumb,
detecting a moderate difference between groups reliably (what statisticians call a “medium
effect size,”  d ≈   0.5 ) needs about 64 people per group at conventional confidence levels —
so this range gives enough participants with some buffer (10–15%) left over for anyone who
drops out, fails an attention check, or doesn’t finish the chat.

5 AnalysisPlan

5.1ComparingtheGroups
Themaincomparisonisthefinalnegotiatedpricebetweenconditions,usingat-test(twocon-
ditions) or ANOVA (three conditions), reporting group averages, confidence intervals, and
effect size. If there are three conditions, follow-up pairwise tests (Tukey’s HSD) show exactly
which pair(s) of conditions differ.

5.2LookingDeeperThanJusttheFinalPrice

We also compare the concession rate(how much of the starting price was given up):

5.3ReadingtheConversationItself
Beyondthefinalnumbers,welookattheshapeofthenegotiation: didtheLLMconcedeearly
and gradually, or hold firm and only concede late?  We also read through transcripts to tag
recognizable behaviors — acknowledging the buyer, making a counteroffer, holding firm, or
reciprocating a concession — so we can describe    how, not just how much, the LLM’s behavior
changed.

5.4MakingSuretheExperimentActuallyWorked(ValidityChecks)

These checks exist to catch a specific risk:  what if a participant assigned to the “empathy”
conditiondoesn’tactuallywriteempatheticmessages,ortheLLMdoesn’tbehaveconsistently?
If that happens, any pattern we find wouldn’t really be about empathy vs. economics at all —
it would just be noise. Two checks guard against this:
 •   Didparticipantsfollowtheirassignedstyle? Weaskthemdirectlyinthepost-survey,but
    self-reportscanbeunreliable,sowealsoreadtheactualchatmessagesourselves(orwitha
    friendhelping)andindependentlyjudgewhetherthelanguageusedmatchestheassigned
    condition,withoutlookingatwhichconditiontheywereassignedtofirst(thisavoidsbias).
    If most of a participant’s messages don’t match their assigned style, that session is flagged
    and can be excluded or analyzed separately.
 •   DidtheLLMbehaveconsistently? Beforecollectingdatafromall100–200participants,we
    run a small trial of about 15–20 test conversations across both conditions and check three
    things: the LLM never agrees to a price below its hidden minimum, it actually negotiates
    back and forth instead of instantly accepting or refusing, and it stays in character as the
    same seller every time. If it fails any of these, we fix the prompt and re-test before running
    the real study — this avoids discovering a broken setup only after collecting all the real
    data.
